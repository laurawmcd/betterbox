# Accessibility

Accessibility covers usability for people with constraints that standard designs overlook, such as colorblindness, poor or no eyesight, limited hearing or limited movement. Designing for them early costs far less than adding it later, and many of the changes help everyone, for example in bright sunlight or on a small screen.

## Questions to ask

- Who among our users has a visual, hearing, motor or cognitive constraint?
- Does any information depend on colour alone?
- Can the system be used with a screen reader or with large text?
- Have we tested with people who have these constraints, and not just against a checklist?

## In a workshop

Pick one screen or message and ask the group to use it with eyes closed or in greyscale. Then discuss what broke.
