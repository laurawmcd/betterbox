# Expectation and norm-setting

Set aside time at the start to ask people what they expect from the workshop and how they want to work together. The exercise makes people feel heard, shows you what the room cares about, and gives you something to point back to later.

**Time:** 10 minutes

## How to run it

1. Put a flipchart where everyone can see it. Ask the group for their expectations and goals for the session, and write them up. You can give people a moment to reflect and then share, or take ideas from the room.
2. Put up a second flipchart and ask for group norms. Explain that for the day you are a team, and that trust depends on behaving in certain ways. Offer an example that fits the room, such as raising a hand rather than interrupting, taking calls outside, or coming back from breaks on time.
3. Tack both charts to the wall and refer to them at key moments, such as just before a break or when someone becomes disruptive.

## Tips

- Expectation-setting carries a risk: people may ask for sessions you have not planned. Note the request visibly and promise to take it back to the organizers. Where a request matches a session you have planned, use it to show why that session matters.
- Norm-setting can feel awkward with a room of adults. It gives you a chance to get ahead of anyone likely to disrupt, and groups that have been through disruption before will often take the opportunity to set the rules themselves.
