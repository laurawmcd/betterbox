# User personas

A persona describes an individual: how they use technology, what their work and home life are like, and what they need from the project. Personas give the group a shared vocabulary for the rest of the day. They also show up inconsistent assumptions about technology and problems in the theory of change, and they speed up the mapping exercises that follow.

**Time:** 30 minutes

## How to run it

1. Prepare prompts on a flipchart or slide: personas describe individuals, how they use technology, and what they need from the tool; who are the users we should talk about?; a sample persona; and 'now write your own'.
2. Put up a flipchart and ask the group to suggest the roles that matter. Look for people who use the system directly and for people who receive its outputs without producing them.
3. In small groups, choose or assign a role and spend 5 to 10 minutes writing a persona on flipchart paper: a name, home life, the technology they use at home and at work, how they get important updates about their job, and what they need that this project could supply.
4. Come back together and spend 10 to 20 minutes talking through the personas. Invite groups to comment on each other's work and amend as you go, so that the whole group owns them.
5. Put the final personas on the wall for the rest of the day.

## Tips

- Prepare one sample persona. Choose a real role that is not central to the system so that the example does not steer the group.
- If people resist group work, run it in plenary, or bring pre-written personas and have the room validate them.
- To validate: put quotes or bullet points on flipcharts and have people put coloured stickers on what they agree and disagree with, or ask someone to read a prepared user story aloud and discuss it.
